Evidence & method

What the film shows.

One real weight edit, a controlled comparison, and a manually commanded mission through victory. This page separates the observed results from the limits of the recording and its review.

Export verified. Browser playback and all ten chapter controls have been observed. Audio has been technically measured; no complete audiovisual watch or perceptual listening review is claimed.

From a weight to an order

The fresh match began paused at 00:00, with Blue under I command. A real coefficient in the authored, untrained auxiliary game policy changed: W2[19], army_train_bias, 0.8 → 24, advancing policy revision 0 → 1.

  1. Edit → preview. The preferred action changed from Train Villager (score 7.86) to Train Spearman (score 25.98). Spearman’s own score rose 2.78 → 25.98. These are preference scores, not probabilities. Preview alone issued no order.
  2. Compare 60 seconds. Two simulations started from the same captured world, using the baseline or edited Blue policy and the same Red policy. The live match stayed at 00:00. Their first different issued decision occurred at +1.6 seconds; they issued 25 versus 26 orders.
  3. Execute → paid queue → completed unit. Run one decision issued Train Spearman. Food fell 500 → 450, wood 300 → 275, and the barracks showed one already-paid recruit with 15 seconds remaining. By 00:48, that recruit had completed and the army numbered three.

A manually commanded victory

Manual gathering, house construction, and seven further recruitment orders produced an army of ten: two starting guards, one policy recruit, and seven manual recruits. A manual attack at 03:30 won at 04:34, with 8 soldiers trained, 3 enemies defeated, and 6 Blue survivors. The weight edit did not autonomously win this battle.

Selected segment 38 images begin after the first of its two manual recruitment purchases. Segment 41 confirms the completed ten-soldier army.

Laya proposed an edit; it was not applied

At 03:30 / tick 2110, native Laya (laya-game, CPU, checkpoint a28adbc55d13) proposed changing attack bias W2[30], −5 → −4. That bounded proposal was not applied: the policy stayed at revision 1. Laya’s checkpoint remained fixed. The demonstration did not train or rewrite it, and its RTS strategy is unvalidated.

How the film was made

The film uses only fresh-run segments 25–43: 181.6 seconds of selected capture, represented by 546 distinct JPEG source files. These are direct screenshots of the controlled game tab, captured at roughly 3 fps.

The 30 fps export repeats the last observed screenshot according to recorded timestamps. It does not contain 30 unique captured images per second or invented intermediate motion. Resumed gameplay used UI 2× speed, with additional editorial acceleration separately labeled. Earlier wrong-tab captures 01–23 are quarantined and excluded.

What was reviewed

Picture and captions

The encoded-frame review covers 16 samples; the production reviewer separately inspected 7, 21, 28, and 88 seconds, for 20 sampled frames total. No material cropping, caption clipping, or readability issue was observed at native resolution. This was not a complete watch.

One minor timing issue remains: at 84 seconds, a destruction caption appears while the enemy town still has 104 health. Victory follows at approximately 85.825 seconds and is visible in the reviewed 88-second frame.

Browser playback and chapters

On the final viewer, clicking Play film started playback at 0.017499 seconds. It progressed to 12.159805 seconds, and clicking Pause film stopped it at 12.320002 seconds, without a media error. All ten chapter buttons were retested by mouse and sought to exactly 0, 4, 11, 17, 25, 33, 40, 59, 70, and 86 seconds, each with the matching explanatory heading, paused state, and no error.

Clicking Play film at 86 seconds advanced to 90.000 seconds, with ended=true, paused=true, and no error; the button changed to Replay film. Clicking Replay film restarted at 0 seconds, with paused=false. Pause film and the baseline chapter then left the player ready at 0 seconds, paused.

The live-game link destination opened in a fresh tab and displayed the original playable mission entry. These checks do not constitute a complete uninterrupted watch or listening review.

Music and measured audio

The soundtrack is “Horizons” by Scott Buckley, under CC BY 4.0. Its prepared 90-second bed retains level adjustment, volume shaping, and fades; gameplay acceleration does not speed up the music. There is no spoken narration or captured game audio.

The final encoded AAC measures −24.02 LUFS integrated, −7.68 dBTP true peak, and 11.20 LU loudness range, with signal in both channels, a quiet opening, and a measured fade-out. These are technical measurements, not a listening review. Musical transitions and subjective playback quality remain unreviewed.
