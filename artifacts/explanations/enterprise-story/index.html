The story, without playback

A telemetry outage should reach Platform Operations. The synthetic baseline sends it to General Support. A direct route could avoid a transfer; this demo does not measure handling time or savings.

  1. Follow the ticket. The router turns structured fields into fixed features. The queue with the highest score wins, if its lead meets the margin rule.
  2. Understand the weight. A weight sets a feature's contribution to a queue score. The recognized outage feature is one for the target and zero on the supplied protection anchors.
  3. See the tradeoff. Target-only fitting improves the outage route but changes protected scores. The ordinary-support record still routes to General Support; a changed score is already a failed protection condition.
  4. Choose a permitted direction. The compiler restricts the edit to directions with zero effect on the fixed-feature protection anchors. In this example it changes only the outage-to-Platform coefficient.
  5. Apply and check. The coefficient changes from 0.25 to 3.22029702970297. Final recognized outage targets route to Platform Operations. Maximum sampled protected score drift is zero.
  6. Keep the limits visible. Conflicting protection causes abstention. An unrecognized symptom causes rejection on the declared final split. These are supplied synthetic splits, not independent customer evidence.
  7. Restore. The bound inverse restores the exact parent artifact. It does not reverse external ticket assignments.

The exact retention claim covers the supplied fixed-feature anchors and their span under real-valued linear semantics. It does not prove floating-point execution, new feature coverage, or text understanding.
