# Cumulative admission and affine bounds: released secondary results

Research date: November 8, 2026, America/Chicago. The coordinated release and
execution receipts use November 9 UTC. Roadmap issues: #10 and #13.

The frozen cumulative controllers admitted no trained proposal in either
domain. Their deployed heads therefore stayed at their original ancestors:
target gain, original protected harm, and whole-sequence useful coverage were
all zero. This demonstrates recorded abstention under the stated contracts;
it provides no trained example of successfully retaining earlier accepted
gains.

The signed affine-box extension produced substantially narrower real bounds
than the triangle calculation, but neither calculation passed the original
score caps. Some fixed BANKING77 proposals helped their requested queues when
evaluated as undeployed candidates. This separates a proposal's empirical task
utility from its score-contract feasibility. The extension changes neither
the native admission policy nor the deployed sequence.

The candidate-before-evaluation boundary was authenticated before execution.
Both evaluations and both separate-process replays completed successfully,
with the externally pinned candidate, protocol, boundary, gate and release
identities unchanged. The replays independently regenerate outcomes in new
processes using the same reviewed implementation. They are not a second
formal implementation or a proof of native arithmetic.

## What was frozen and what was reused

[The cumulative design](cumulative-admission.md) binds each step to the
original head, its current parent, and every accepted earlier insertion. Its
local controller enforces current-parent obligations; its cumulative
controller additionally enforces original and earlier-insertion obligations.
Both keep rejected candidates in the ledger and deploy the last accepted
checkpoint. Both use the same score cap fixed from the original model, with
no later enlargement.

[The affine design](affine-bounds.md) compares the signed and triangle bounds
on identical captured candidate/parent pairs, feature boxes and score caps.
It evaluates these fixed proposals even when admission rejected them. A
bound-status category refers to the frozen calibration comparison; heldout
utility is a separate endpoint.

Each domain has six independently trained full lineages. Forward and reverse
orders and the two controllers share their trained ancestor and request
sample. BANKING77 has four requests per sequence, including ownership changes
and recognition requests; optical digits has two. These are instances of the
generic readout contract, with distinct task interpretations.

No new model training, request acquisition, candidate selection or deployment
occurred in this evaluation. Primary rows are the previously acquired novel
official-test features; complete official-test features provide secondary
sensitivity evidence. The earlier study already used these rows, so this is
an adjacent secondary estimation study, not fresh population confirmation.
Every assigned sequence and fixed proposal remains in the denominator.

## Cumulative admission: no trained sequence was useful

Joint usefulness requires every final target gain to be at least 0.10,
original protected net harm throughout to be at most 0.01, and earlier accepted
targets' loss from their insertion checkpoints throughout to be at most 0.01.
These utility requirements remain distinct from exact numerical score gates.

| Domain | Controller | Assigned sequences | Admitted steps | Rejected steps | Missing primary sequences | Whole-sequence useful coverage |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BANKING77 | Local | 12 | 0 | 48 | 0 | 0% |
| BANKING77 | Cumulative | 12 | 0 | 48 | 0 | 0% |
| Optical digits | Local | 12 | 0 | 24 | 0 | 0% |
| Optical digits | Cumulative | 12 | 0 | 24 | 0 | 0% |

These denominators account for assigned work; they are not measures of
enterprise impact. There were no proposal failures disguised as missing
outcomes and no no-op candidates. All named rollback receipts replayed.

For both controllers in both domains, mean final target gain and mean final
original protected harm are zero on both primary and complete-test endpoints.
Their six-lineage descriptive Student-t intervals are `[0, 0]`; the paired
cumulative-minus-local gain, harm and useful-coverage differences are also
zero with those intervals. The interval is mechanically degenerate because
all deployed endpoints are unchanged. It establishes no general guarantee
about future requests, successful edits, or population harm.

The matrices preserve every rejected step, order, endpoint hash, target
accuracy and original-baseline comparison. With no accepted insertion, there
is no empirical insertion-age trajectory or retained-gain decay to estimate.
Zero maximum insertion loss is therefore vacuous here; it is not evidence of
successful continual learning.

The separately constructed boundary chain remains useful engineering
evidence. Its relevant increments pass local gates while the accumulated
endpoint violates the original score cap; the cumulative controller rejects
that endpoint. Replay also checks conflicts, reconstruction of retained
insertions, and named inverse composition. Constructed falsifiers establish
policy behavior on those inputs, not a trained distributional advantage.

Rollback covers only the captured readout artifact and its immutable interface
metadata. The receipts explicitly exclude encoder mutation, caches, RNG,
queue assignments, the deployment process and already executed business
actions. In this trained evaluation an unchanged endpoint still has a named
rollback receipt; the report does not describe it as undoing external work.

## Affine bounds: tighter envelopes, unchanged score-cap feasibility

The signed affine interval uses coefficient signs to compute exact extrema
on the declared real feature box. The triangle calculation loses cancellation
and sign information. The median signed/triangle maximum-bound ratio over
distinct frozen pairs is 0.5014 for BANKING77 and 0.5045 for digits, as measured
in the original calibration comparison.

| Frozen calibration category | BANKING77 assigned proposals | Optical digits assigned proposals |
|---|---:|---:|
| Distinct captured endpoint/domain pairs | 24 | 12 |
| Assigned records, including repeated orders/controllers | 96 | 48 |
| Signed score-cap passes | 0 | 0 |
| Triangle score-cap passes | 0 | 0 |
| Observed calibration score violations | 72 | 24 |
| Rejected bounds with sampled calibration residual within cap | 24 | 24 |

The last two rows partition the assigned records for both calculations.
`conservative_rejection` means the finite calibration sample did not exhibit
the violation; it does not mean the exact signed box extrema are loose. If
the signed maximum exceeds the cap, an attaining box corner violates the
declared real contract. A tighter scalar calculation on the same affine
equation and box cannot eliminate that corner.

for gain, harm and flips. Brackets are descriptive 95% Student-t intervals
over six lineage means, conditional on the shared fixed request sample.

Proposal utility requires target queue gain at least 0.10, protected local
queue flips at most 0.01 and protected local net harm at most 0.01. It compares
the candidate to its actual parent rather than substituting an unexecuted
ideal edit. BANKING77's 50% utility coverage is identical inside all six
lineages, producing the descriptive `[0.5, 0.5]` interval. This is not an exact
population coverage bound. No score-cap pass plus utility pass occurred.

On the complete-test sensitivity endpoint, BANKING77 mean target gain is
13.2292 percentage points `[10.4645, 15.9938]`, protected harm is 0.0528
`[0.0423, 0.0633]`, and protected flips are 0.0656 `[0.0454, 0.0859]`.
Utility coverage and both bound/usefulness intersections remain unchanged.
The digits primary and complete-test summaries coincide because their
declared populations coincide in this evidence bundle.

The exact in-box observer found no signed per-output real-bound violation on
the supplied heldout feature rows. It lifts stored endpoints and features
into exact binary rationals and directly subtracts endpoints without calling
the signed generator. This supports correspondence on those observations;
it is separate from the extrema argument for the full box.

Both domains also contain out-of-box heldout rows. Such rows lack the
certificate's premise. Neither their behavior nor native numerical
exceedances are reported as a real certificate violation. Native score-cap
exceedances occur in both domains, while no observed native signed-bound
exceedance is recorded. Native observations use CPU float32 affine scores
and binary64 differences, with comparison against binary64 projections of
real bounds. No floating execution refinement is inferred from these checks.

The original calibration timing and traced-allocation results remain in
[the affine report](affine-bounds.md). They cover one ordered run per distinct
pair, with `tracemalloc`, and exclude process RSS, native/kernel memory, GPUs
and full enterprise deployment costs. The released heldout evaluation does
not turn them into a repeated performance or energy benchmark.

## Interpretation and next experiment

The cumulative mechanism enforces a meaningful original and old-target
contract on constructed boundary examples. The frozen trained workload
provides only abstention and unchanged-head observations. A later proposer
study must obtain admissible, useful candidates before these lineages can
answer whether cumulative admission protects earlier gains better than local
admission.

The signed extension closes a real semantic-registry gap and measures a
smaller envelope. It does not solve this workload's score-cap bottleneck.
Proposal control, correlated feature domains and input-to-feature guarantees
are distinct future experiments. They must receive new frozen designs rather
than enlarge caps, shrink boxes or reselect candidates in this study.

The four extension trust flags remain false: implementation machine checking,
feature-extractor verification, floating execution verification and core
native-admission integration. Attention, normalization, stochastic/stateful
operators and arbitrary backend kernels inherit no guarantee from the
affine theorem. The current plain proof and independent checker apply only
to their declared real subset.

The resource-capped six-lineage design supports descriptive estimation. Even
six perfect binary lineage outcomes would have a two-sided exact 95% lower
bound of about 0.5407, below the requested 0.75 coverage threshold. That
statistical limit does not require an unfunded new study; it limits the claim
made from the current one. Here the measured useful bound/admission coverage
is zero anyway.
